# Combat

See `Story Bible.md` for why combat is structured this way (fists first, cards as taught/borrowed power).

## Basic actions vs. cards

Every fight, from your very first, gives you two free actions with no deck required:
- **Attack** — modest, reliable damage.
- **Defend** — mitigate/reduce incoming damage.

On top of that, you can spend **Focus** (a small resource, e.g. 2-3, refilling each turn) to play cards from your hand instead. Cards hit harder or apply elemental/special effects, but are limited by both your Focus and your hand/deck size — you're never forced to play a card just because it's in your hand, so you can hoard Focus/cards for a tougher fight (namely, a cave's bottom-of-dive boss) instead of dumping everything into a trash mob.

You're limited to initially 10 cards total in your deck before having to shuffle, with three cards in your hand at a time. Playing a card draws a replacement from the deck; when the deck runs out, the discard pile reshuffles back in.

## Starting deck vs. taught cards

Your starting deck is the plain playing-card-named cards (Ace of Diamonds, Three of Hearts, etc.) — mundane, untaught, "yours" before anyone showed you anything. The elemental archetypes are what you get taught:

Currently the **Card Archetypes** are As Follows
- Fire
- Acid
- Light
- Ice
- Lightning

Per the Story Bible, each of these is unlocked by rescuing the NPC from a cave who teaches it to you — replacing or upgrading a starting playing-card with its elemental counterpart. A fresh run starts close to fists-only and grows into a full elemental deck as the town grows, so combat complexity should track narrative progress rather than being available all at once.

## Enemy design

We would like to purposefully scale the enemy to only really last three turns before dieing as to not make the game drawn out.
To add some depth to the combat though, we would like to do some simple things when creating the combat creatures
- Making some Creatures resistant to certain attacks. IE, a skeleton is resistant to fire (nothing to burn) but is weak to Acid.
- A Jelly is succeptable to Lightning, but resistant to Acid. 
- Etc...

This way, your not just playing any card and winning, there is some trial and error. Per the Story Bible's fractal cave structure, a given cave's shallow rooms should reuse/reflavor the existing "shadow" roster, its deeper rooms should field tougher remixed variants, and its bottom-of-dive boss should be hand-authored for that specific cave.

Combat will be Turn Based, as in you perform an action and then the enemy performs an action.
